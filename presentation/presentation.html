A shared climate signal

Budbreak advances ~5.8 days per °C of dormant-season warming — and in cool climates it runs into a phenology–temperature mismatch: a bump in freeze-damage risk where budbreak moves earlier faster than late-frost risk falls.

rhAmpSeq and Flex-Seq: two answers to marker transferability

rhAmpSeq · VitisFlex-Seq · cranberry
Design targetCollinear core genome — 39.8 Mb, ~8.7% of the genome, syntenic across 10 Vitis assembliesWhole genome, uniform ~27 kb spacing, from an 11-accession single-species pangenome
Design goalTransferability across the genus — wild species carry the resistance lociWithin-species density — fine-mapping, GWAS, germplasm forensics
Markers2,000 amplicons (270–330 bp)17,502 amplicons (219 bp ± 109)
Marker typeMulti-allelic haplotypes · 5.7 alleles / marker from 7 parentsMulti-allelic microhaplotypes · 6.1 / locus from 192 accessions
Validation91.9% of markers recovered across 4 diverse interspecific families99.8% of loci recovered in ≥90% of a single-species panel
Deployment>80,000 vines genotyped; feeds KASP markers for routine MAS (VitisGen3)New (2026); pairs with the public DArTag panel (3,059 loci) for routine work
The two recovery figures are not comparable — across a genus is the harder test. And “core” means different things: collinear across species (rhAmpSeq) vs present in every accession (pangenome).

Q&A only. Use if asked "would you build a Flex-Seq panel for grape?" or "how does this relate to rhAmpSeq?"

  • Same class of technology (targeted amplicon sequencing, reference-anchored panel, haplotype calls). The difference that matters is not chemistry but what fraction of the genome was targeted, and why: rhAmpSeq deliberately gave up ~91% of the genome to work in V. vinifera, riparia, rupestris, cinerea, muscadine and every hybrid between them; cranberry could afford whole-genome density because it is one species with a narrow gene pool — and paid for it with 45% missing SNP data in V. oxycoccos.
  • Nearly the same alleles per marker (5.7 vs 6.1) from a 27× difference in sampling (14 vs 384 chromosomes) — that is Vitis interspecific diversity vs cranberry's post-1800s bottleneck, not a platform difference.
  • Answer to "would you build it": no — rhAmpSeq exists, is validated at scale, and is already tiered into KASP. The open question is whether a higher-density, microhaplotype layer earns its per-sample cost for specific jobs: fine-mapping in a target population, or germplasm identity / clonal-purity audits. That would be a conversation with Lance, not a proposal.
  • Terminology trap: if you say "core", specify which sense. rhAmpSeq's "collinear core genome" is about synteny across species; Flex-Seq's "core genes" is pangenome occupancy (Yocca et al. 2023). A gene can be one without the other.
  • Chemistry, one sentence if pushed: rhAmpSeq gates primer extension enzymatically (RNase H2); Flex-Seq uses two flanking probes; DArTag a single MIP-style oligo. Stop there.
